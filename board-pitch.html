The Case · 1 of 3

Why now

Analysts already spend ~$900/month per desk on outside AI tools—pasting CoinDesk articles into Claude to write memos and checking SEC links in Perplexity.
ChatGPT and Perplexity can summarize public articles, but they cannot see CoinDesk Indices (CDI) or our reporters' scoops before publication. We have 12 institutional firms ready to test a 4-week pilot in Week 1, giving us a clear window to own this workflow before generic chatbots catch up.
The Case · 2 of 3

Why we won't get copied in 6 months

Our edge is exclusive price data + newsroom speed paired with human-verified compliance edits.
If we automatically train the AI on every user edit, bad fixes will corrupt answers or leak client secrets. Instead, when a compliance officer fixes a legal summary, we save it in a private client queue. CoinDesk’s legal analysts verify the fix within 24 hours before adding it to our master database—building a trusted library of crypto-legal answers OpenAI cannot scrape.

Risks & Controls

What could go wrong

1. Legal Hallucinations
A wrong regulatory summary could get a client in trouble. We test every release against 150 ground-truth questions (<1% false facts), link every claim to a primary source, and block outputs completely if confidence is under 50%.
2. Slowdowns at 10x Scale
Traffic spikes during market crashes could push generation past our 1-minute speed limit. Medium-confidence briefs (50%–75%) show a yellow warning badge immediately while a human checks them in the background. Every prompt and output is saved in uneditable SEC Rule 17a-4 logs.
3. When We Walk Away
During our 4-week pilot with 12 firms, if analysts reject the briefs (<80% acceptance) or speed drops past 1 minute during volatile markets, we kill the standalone web app and sell the backend as a simple data API for Bloomberg and Refinitiv.
