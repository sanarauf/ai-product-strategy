The Case · 1 of 3

Why now

Our own customers are already finishing our work in other AI tools.
We audited 12 existing terminal accounts and found 6 workarounds. Analysts paste our briefs into Claude to build investment committee memos, check our SEC citations in Perplexity, run our briefs through scripts to estimate portfolio risk, and route our alerts through ChatGPT to Telegram. That's about $900 a month of outside AI spend across the seats we surveyed, and client research is leaving our product. Meanwhile, OpenAI's research tools are an estimated 3–6 months from making generic crypto news summaries free, which puts about 60% of our current value at risk.
The Case · 2 of 3

What's defensible

Our edge today is our data. The second edge, learning from customer corrections, is still being built.
CoinDesk index prices and newsroom reporting reach a brief within 1.5 seconds, with a source attached to every claim. Generic AI tools only see news after it's published and indexed on the open web. The second defense comes from compliance officers who fix a brief: a CoinDesk legal analyst reviews each fix within 24 hours, and approved fixes improve future answers. That loop scores 2 out of 5 today and is a 3–9 month build. Overall, our data is strong; learning from users and across clients is still weak.

The economics

80% gross margin, down from 95%. Running the AI costs about $165 per user per month.
About 65% of requests go to cheaper, faster AI models; only complex regulatory analysis uses the most capable ones. If AI prices triple, moving more work to cheaper models keeps margin around 67%. If one provider raises prices 50%, routine work moves to open-source models at a cost of about 3 points of margin. Open issue: the proposed $4,500 a month for a 5-person team is less than those 5 seats pay today ($12,500). We test what customers will pay during the pilot before changing pricing.

The Risks

What could break this

Trust / Failure
The front-page story is a fund acting on a brief that gets a regulation wrong during a crash. Every claim links to its source. We target 92% accuracy and under 1% made-up facts, and we roll back automatically above 2%. Briefs under 60% confidence go to a human analyst; under 50% aren't generated at all. The product never places trades. Our test set has 20 cases today; 150 is a 3-month deliverable.
Scale / Governance
At 10 times today's usage, human review becomes the bottleneck. Crypto crashes happen on nights and weekends, when review currently falls to a customer success manager, not an analyst. Uncertain briefs go to a separate queue so everything else still arrives in under a minute, and we'll train after-hours reviewers before scaling. Every question and answer is stored permanently for SEC record-keeping, client data never trains the AI, and EU AI Act paperwork comes before European sales.
Competitive
If OpenAI or Messari offer briefs good enough, for free or cheaper, that our pilot analysts don't prefer ours, the standalone product loses. Kill criteria: fewer than 80% of pilot analysts accept the briefs, regulatory errors we can't fix, or briefs taking over a minute in a fast market. If any hits, we stop building the terminal and sell our data and briefs through Bloomberg and Refinitiv instead.
